# ONE — Behavioral Science & Psychological Research

> **Evidence before gimmicks.** We ground all behavioral interventions in peer-reviewed cognitive science, neuroscience, and attention research.

## 1. Attention Fragmentation & The Interruption Penalty

### Dr. Gloria Mark (UC Irvine) — Attention Switching & Recovery
* **Key Finding:** After an external or internal interruption, knowledge workers take an average of **23 minutes and 15 seconds** to return to their original focused task.
* **Secondary Effect:** People compensate for interruptions by working faster, which produces significantly higher levels of stress, frustration, mental effort, and perceived time pressure.
* **Product Implication for ONE:** The primary goal is not preventing 100% of interruptions, but shortening the latency between an interruption and returning to the task. Shorter recovery loops prevent the 23-minute drift.

## 2. Habit Loops, Automaticity, and Micro-Friction

### Dual-Process Theory (Kahneman) & Habit Architecture (Wood & Neal)
* **Habitual Trajectory:** Digital distractions (e.g. tapping Instagram or opening Reddit in a new tab) operate almost entirely in **System 1** (fast, automatic, unconscious muscle memory).
* **PNAS 2023 Study (Riedel, Yasseri, et al. on "one sec"):**
  * Introducing a brief 10-second micro-friction pause combined with an explicit option to dismiss decreased target app openings by **57%**.
  * The friction acts as a cognitive wedge, forcing conscious **System 2** deliberation.
* **Product Implication for ONE:** When a distraction trigger occurs during ONE THING MODE, the **Intent Firewall** inserts deliberate friction:
  *"WAIT. You said: [Goal]. 42 minutes remain. What happened?"*
  This immediately transforms an unconscious motor reflex into conscious choice.

## 3. The "What-the-Hell" Effect & Shame-Free Self-Regulation

### Polivy & Herman (Dietary & Behavioral Disinhibition)
* **Psychological Mechanism:** When individuals trying to maintain strict discipline violate a self-imposed rule, they perceive their goal as "ruined." This produces guilt, demoralization, and immediate complete surrender to the temptation (the "What-the-Hell" effect).
* **Dr. Kristin Neff (Self-Compassion in Goal Pursuit):** Self-compassion fosters greater emotional resilience and faster recovery from behavioral lapses than self-criticism or punitive tracking.
* **Product Implication for ONE:**
  * Eliminate streak counters that reset to zero on a missed day.
  * Never use failure vocabulary (*"Failed"*, *"Broken"*, *"Wasted"*).
  * Use restorative prompts: *"You drifted. Come back. Let's restart."*
  * **Focus Rescue ("Rescue Me"):** *"You haven't lost the day. What needs your attention now?"*

## 4. Working Memory & The Zeigarnik Effect

### Bluma Zeigarnik & Cognitive Offloading
* **The Zeigarnik Effect:** Incomplete tasks and unresolved thoughts persist in working memory, continuously consuming cognitive bandwidth and generating attention intrusions.
* **Product Implication for ONE — Distraction Inbox:**
  * When a user thinks *"I need to order groceries"* or *"Look up that library"*, attempting to forcefully suppress the thought creates cognitive load.
  * ONE provides **instant capture** (`Cmd/Ctrl+K` or quick button): *"Save for Later"*.
  * The thought is offloaded to a persistent inbox, signaling the brain that it is safe to return to the active intention.

## 5. Implementation Intentions

### Dr. Peter Gollwitzer (NYU)
* **Formula:** "If [Trigger], then I will [Planned Action]."
* **Evidence:** Forming explicit implementation intentions doubles the rate of goal achievement compared to vague aspirations.
* **Product Implication for ONE:** When setting up a session, ONE prompts the user to state their exact outcome (*"Finish authentication architecture"*) rather than a broad subject (*"Work on project"*).
